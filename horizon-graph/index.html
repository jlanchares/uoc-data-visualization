    How to read this chart

    A Horizon Graph reduces vertical space by dividing values into magnitude bands and overlaying them. This makes comparisons across multiple time series easier, at the cost of making individual values harder to estimate without a tooltip.

    Blue shows growth and rises from the bottom edge of a row. Red shows contraction and hangs from the top edge, so both edges of a row mark 0%. Each row folds the same three bands into one height: when a value passes the top of a band, the next darker shade starts again from the same edge. Darker therefore always means a larger change, in either direction.

    Red in the same part of several rows marks a downturn shared by several countries, such as 2009 (five of the six) and 2020 (all six). Rows that change shade often are more volatile, and the blue after a red section shows how each country recovered. The chart describes what happened and does not explain why.

    Intended audience: people interested in comparative macroeconomic trends.
